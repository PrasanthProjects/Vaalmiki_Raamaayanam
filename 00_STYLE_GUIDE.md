# శ్రీమద్రామాయణం — VISUAL STYLE GUIDE
### Era, region, architecture, clothing, palette — the baseline every character and scene sits on top of

This file is the third leg of the consistency system, alongside `00_CHARACTER_BIBLE.md` (who each character is) and `00_INDEX.md` (what's been scripted). Every episode's visual prompts should carry both a character's **Stage tag** from the Character Bible AND a **location register** from this file.

---

## Rendering style — realism (applies to every single scene, no exceptions)

**Every shot must be photorealistic/cinematic, not flat illustration, anime, cartoon, or painted-storybook style.** This is a standing rule from the very start of this project (scenes must be "very good, realistic and natural") and applies uniformly across all ~250+ episodes and every short — no episode or Khaanda gets a different art style. Concretely, every visual prompt should carry language like: *"photorealistic, cinematic lighting, natural skin texture and cloth movement, live-action film quality, shot on a real camera lens (35mm/50mm equivalent), naturalistic depth of field"* — and should explicitly exclude: flat 2D illustration, anime/manga style, cartoon proportions, painted/watercolor storybook look, or an overly glossy "3D render" plastic look. Reference-grade target: it should look like a big-budget live-action historical film, not an animated adaptation.

This applies to characters AND environments equally — the South Indian temple-era architecture and clothing described below should look like real, physically-built sets and real woven fabric under natural or firelight lighting, not stylized digital-painting backdrops. Crowd scenes, forest scenes, and divine/mythic-scale moments (Shiva drinking poison, the Ganga's descent, etc.) still follow this same realism rule — awe and scale should come from cinematic composition and lighting, not from switching to a more stylized/illustrative rendering mode.

## Era baseline

**Treta Yuga, South Indian visual register.** No medieval North-Indian/Mughal-era motifs — no domes, no jaali lattice screens, no Persian-style turbans. Reference early South Indian temple-era iconography (Chola/Vijayanagara-adjacent visual language) adapted to a pre-classical Vedic-kingdom setting, not literal medieval temple architecture either — this is meant to read as *older* than that, a founding-myth era.

## Architecture by location

- **Ayodhya palace:** multi-tiered wooden-and-stone gopuram-style gateways, pillared mandapas, sloped tiled roofs, courtyards with tulasi/yagna platforms. Warm sandstone and teakwood tones.
- **Mithila (Janaka's court):** similar register, but simpler and more agrarian-prosperous — less military ornamentation than Ayodhya.
- **Forest ashrams** (Vishwamitra, Bharadwaja, Atri, Agastya, Sabari/Shabari, Sharabhanga): thatched-roof huts, mud-and-thatch construction, a sacred fire (agni-kunda) always present, deer skins, kamandalu, dense forest/riverbank surroundings.
- **Panchavati hut** (Rama/Sita/Lakshmana's exile home): small bamboo-and-leaf structure, minimal, no ornamentation, riverside.
- **Kishkindha:** cave-and-rock vanara settlements, natural boulders, banyan trees — no built architecture, organic and wild.
- **Lanka:** the one deliberate visual contrast — darker stone, taller and more imposing towers, gold-heavy ostentatious ornamentation, ocean-facing ramparts. Still Dravidian-temple derived, never demonic/gothic — the text describes Ravana's Lanka as fabulously wealthy, not ugly.

## Clothing

- **Men (royalty/students):** dhoti (veshti) — silk with gold-zari border for royalty, plain cotton for commoners/hermits; angavastram draped over one shoulder; bare-chested or a thin upper cloth in forest settings.
- **Men (exile/hermit):** valkala (bark-fiber cloth), reddish-ochre/tan tone; deer-skin (krishnajina) worn by sages during rituals.
- **Women (royalty):** saree/antariya in an early Dravidian drape — a single-piece wrap with simply-tucked pleats, NOT the modern Nivi drape (no pallu-over-shoulder modern styling) — rich silk with woven borders (kanchipuram-adjacent motif), minimal stitched clothing.
- **Women (exile):** same valkala treatment as men, simplified.
- **Jewelry:** kadiyam (bangles), jhumka/stud earrings, waist belts (ordanam) for queens, rudraksha/tulasi strands for ascetics — gold-heavy for royalty, absent-to-minimal in exile stages.
- **Hair:** women in long single braids, often with flowers (jasmine/lotus); men with a topknot (kudumi) as boys, jeweled hair as princes, jataa (matted locks) for sages and for Rama/Lakshmana/Sita once in exile.

## Kiratas / forest & tribal peoples

Kiratas, Nishadas (Guha's people), and other forest-dwelling tribes: darker, sun-weathered skin, minimal clothing (short wrap-cloths), bow-and-arrow as the primary tool, simple bead/shell/bone jewelry — no woven-silk elements at all. Hair often tied back loosely or kept short. Visually distinct from BOTH royal Ayodhya dress and sage/hermit dress. Guha, as Nishada king, gets a step up in ornament (chief-level) but stays within this tribal register — never royal silk.

## Vanaras (Kishkindha / Sundara / Yuddha Khaandas)

Anthropomorphic monkey-form throughout — don't drift toward either fully human or fully animal across scenes. Fur tone varies by named character for recognition (see Character Bible: Hanuman reddish-gold, Sugriva tawny-brown, Vali darker brown-black, Tara golden/warm-toned, Jambavantha grey — he's a bear/riksha, not a monkey). Body proportion and level of anthropomorphism (upright stance, expressive hands/face, minimal waist-cloth) stays constant for all vanaras across every Khaanda they appear in.

## Rakshasas (Aranya / Sundara / Yuddha Khaandas)

Register scales by narrative role:
- Rank-and-file Lankan soldiers: muscular, dark-toned, fanged but humanoid, armored.
- Named rakshasas (Ravana, Kumbhakarna, Indrajit, Vibhishana): individualized, consistent designs per their own Character Bible locks.
- Explicitly monstrous/grotesque rakshasas (per the text's own description — e.g. during Sita's captivity intimidation scenes, or Viradha/Kabandha/Ayomukhi): can be more exaggerated.
- Vibhishana, once he defects, should read visually closer to the Ayodhya register (restrained ornament, calmer posture) to mark his moral shift — this is already in his Character Bible entry, repeated here as the governing style-guide principle behind it.

## Palette by location register

| Register | Palette | Mood |
|---|---|---|
| Ayodhya / Mithila | Warm gold, cream, deep red, sandstone | Prosperity, order |
| Forest / exile | Ochre, forest green, river-blue, bark-brown | Austerity, nature |
| Kishkindha | Earthy browns, banyan-green, rock-grey | Wild, organic |
| Lanka | Deep gold, black-stone, ocean-teal, blood-red accents | Opulence with menace |

## Cinematic & devotional treatment — the house language for recurring shot types

These defaults apply to every episode wherever a row names the shot type below. A row's own visual prompt adds to them; it never needs to repeat them. Everything stays under the realism rule above: devotion comes from light, composition, pace and music — never from switching to an illustrated or cartoon look.

**Rama's presence (darshan shots).** Whenever the text dwells on Rama himself — being seen, recognised, praised, or blessing someone — frame him slightly below eye level, a slow push-in, soft warm key light from one side and a faint rim of light behind his head and shoulders (natural sunlight or firelight, never a painted halo). Hold still for a beat longer than a normal cut. Background out of focus. No music stings — a sustained tanpura/veena drone.

**Divine revelation moments** (Hanuman seeing Rama as Vishnu, the gods watching the Rama-Bharata debate, Brahma rising to greet Sharabhanga, Jatayu's and Shabari's ascents): a brief, restrained overlay of light — golden-white bloom, drifting motes, a gentle lens flare — lasting under two seconds, then back to the real scene. The humans in frame react with stillness and folded hands, never with spectacle-movie gasps.

**Shloka cards** (rows whose visual begins "On-screen shloka text"): the shloka in clean Telugu script, cream-gold letters with a very slight shadow, centred in the lower two-thirds, over the named image slowed to near-stillness (a 2-3% push-in). A faint oil-lamp flicker in the grade. Audio: the narrator recites over a low veena or tanpura; no percussion. The text fades in line by line with the recitation and holds a full beat after the last word.

**Recap lines** (rows whose visual begins "Recap"): reuse the final shot of the previous episode, desaturated by about 15% for the first two seconds, then warming back to full colour as the narration hands over to the new episode.

**End cards** (rows whose visual is "End card"): a slow fade to the channel's warm dark background with the next episode's line as typeset Telugu text, per `00_INTRO_OUTRO_TEMPLATES.md`; a single temple-bell tone at the fade.

**Grief and lament** (Rama at Pampa and the empty hut, Kausalya at the forest shraddha, Jatayu's death): handheld-steady camera at eye level, natural overcast or dusk light, long takes; let silence and ambient sound (wind, river, birds) carry the moment before the narration resumes.

**Counsel and dharma dialogue** (Rama-Bharata, Lakshmana's counsel, Hanuman's speeches, Maricha's warning): two-shots and over-the-shoulder coverage in natural light; the listener's reaction is as important as the speaker — cut to it on the key line.

**Nature as witness** (the Godavari's silence, trees stilling at Ravana's approach, animals pointing south, the three left eyes twitching): let the landscape itself act — wind dropping, water slowing, sound thinning out — photographed for real, not animated.

**Violence** follows the per-episode guidance: wide, fast, non-graphic, aftermath at a distance; the emotional weight goes to faces, not wounds.

## How this connects to the other two files

Every episode script's visual-prompt column should carry, for each shot: **a character's Stage tag** (from `00_CHARACTER_BIBLE.md` — e.g. "Rama Visual Lock, S4/exile stage") **+ a location register** (from this file — e.g. "Panchavati hut, forest-green palette"). Neither file alone is enough — the Character Bible tells an AI tool what a person looks like, this file tells it what the *world* around them looks like, and together they're what keeps two videos generated a week apart, by two different prompts, still looking like the same show.

*See `00_INDEX.md` for the numbering scheme and episode-by-episode progress; see `00_CHARACTER_BIBLE.md` for every named character's Visual Lock and Voice Profile.*
